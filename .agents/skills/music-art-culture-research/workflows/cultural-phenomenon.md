# Cultural Phenomenon Research Workflow

Use for genres, scenes, fandoms, rituals, trends, subcultures, platform practices, cultural movements, and other social-cultural phenomena.

## Procedure
1. Define the phenomenon and competing definitions.
2. Establish chronology and geography.
3. Identify actors, communities, institutions, infrastructures, technologies, and economic incentives.
4. Examine production, circulation, mediation, participation, and reception.
5. Compare emic/insider accounts with etic/analytical interpretations.
6. Search for counterexamples, missing archives, marginalized voices, and selection effects.
7. Test explanations against alternative mechanisms.
8. Connect micro practices, meso institutions, and macro structures only when evidence supports the connection.
9. Compare cases when useful and make case-selection logic explicit.
10. Separate correlation, mechanism, and causation.
11. End with the strongest explanation, alternatives, limitations, and evidence gaps.

## Analytical dimensions

| Dimension | Questions |
|---|---|
| Production | Who creates, funds, organizes, or controls the cultural object/practice? |
| Circulation | How does it move through labels, venues, platforms, institutions, or networks? |
| Participation | Who participates, and under what conditions? |
| Mediation | Which technologies, media, algorithms, critics, or institutions shape visibility? |
| Reception | How do audiences interpret, use, contest, or transform it? |
| Economy | What incentives, markets, labor structures, and business models matter? |
| Power | Which institutions, groups, or infrastructures shape access and legitimacy? |
| Change | What mechanisms explain emergence, persistence, transformation, or decline? |

## Output
Produce definition, timeline, actors/institutions, evidence, competing explanations, analysis, limitations, conclusion, and source list.
