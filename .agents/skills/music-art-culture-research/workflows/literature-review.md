# Literature Review Workflow

Use for literature reviews, systematic reviews, scoping reviews, and research-gap mapping.

## Steps
1. Define the review question, scope, period, geography, and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
2. Generate synonyms, historical terminology, related concepts, and competing explanations.
3. Search multiple scholarly discovery systems available in the environment.
4. Record database/tool, exact query, date, filters, and result counts when available.
5. Normalize metadata and deduplicate by DOI, then normalized title + author + year.
6. Screen titles/abstracts and record inclusion/exclusion decisions and reasons.
7. Retrieve full text when legitimately available; label abstract/snippet-only evidence.
8. Start from strong reviews and landmark works, then trace references backward and citations forward.
9. Extract question, object/sample, method, evidence, findings, limitations, and relevance.
10. Group literature by themes, methods, schools of thought, chronology, or competing explanations.
11. Compare agreement, disagreement, methodological differences, and changes over time.
12. Identify genuine gaps only after checking terminology, language, database, access, and disciplinary boundaries.
13. Draft a synthesis rather than a source-by-source list.
14. Audit claims against the evidence before finalizing.

## Final checks

- Do not claim comprehensiveness unless the protocol supports it.
- Abstract-only evidence is labeled as such.
- Citation counts are discovery signals, not quality scores.
- Competing explanations and negative evidence are included.
- Research gaps are evidence-based, not rhetorical.
